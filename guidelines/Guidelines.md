# Rentre interface guidelines

Rentre is a Canada-focused rental platform. Its landlord workspace should feel like a calm, data-dense property operations tool: practical, editorial, and assured rather than generic SaaS.

## Visual language

- Use the warm parchment ground and cream cards with forest-green as the primary action color. Reserve burnt orange for priority, paid unlocks, and time-sensitive leasing signals.
- Use Fraunces for display headings, DM Sans for interface copy, and JetBrains Mono only for prices, counts, scores, and structured labels.
- Prefer thin warm-gray rules, compact status chips, and measured card radii. Keep utility actions visually quiet.
- Use small uppercase mono or sans labels with positive tracking for metadata. Body copy should remain clear and high contrast.

## Rental workflows

- Preserve renter anonymity through discovery, viewing requests, and offers. Make the cost and consequence of a paid reveal explicit before it occurs.
- In landlord views, separate unit status, prospect activity, and next action. A vacant unit with prospects should lead to profile review; a vacant unit without prospects should lead to listing.
- Treat Equifax credit reports, full profiles, and identity reveals as progressive disclosure, never as automatically-visible data.

## Interaction

- Make cards with a unit-level purpose openable and keyboard accessible. Nested row actions must not accidentally trigger the card action.
- Use bottom sheets for focused mobile-friendly workflows, with a clear image-led context header and an obvious close control.
- Keep responsive layouts intentional: dense data grids may expand on larger screens and collapse to two columns on narrow screens.
